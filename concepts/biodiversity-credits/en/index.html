Biodiversity credits生物多样性信用

A measurable, independently verified, tradable unit of durable and additional net positive biodiversity gain that funds conservation or restoration rather than offsetting damage elsewhere.

“…a certificate that represents a measured and evidence-based unit of positive biodiversity outcome that is durable and additional to what would have otherwise occurred.” BCA 2024 · Wunder et al. 2025, Bus Strategy Environ 34:8752–8767 · Reviews Wauchope et al. 2024 · Peng et al. 2024, 2026

Criteria

Necessary & Jointly Required

  • Demonstrates genuine net positive biodiversity gain above a counterfactual baseline (Additionality)
  • Measured via multi-taxa field monitoring and audited by an accredited independent third party (Verification)
  • Backed by long-term stewardship contracts and a risk buffer pool against reversal (Durability / Permanence)
  • Used for voluntary positive nature contributions and community stewardship, never to license habitat destruction elsewhere (Non-offsetting)

Common, Not Required

  • Bundled or stacked with carbon credits
  • Marketed around a single charismatic flagship species
  • Issued in globally uniform area or time denominations

Controversies

Fungibility vs. Ecological Irreplaceability

Unlike carbon (where 1 tCO₂e is globally interchangeable), biodiversity is spatially heterogeneous and non-fungible. Standardizing units into cross-biome percentages risks adverse selection against slow-recovering alpine or dryland ecosystems, whereas retaining full local specificity limits market liquidity.

Wauchope et al. 2024 · Wunder et al. 2025
Restoration Gains vs. Climax Stewardship

Degraded sites exhibit rapid early metric gains, whereas intact old-growth or restored climax communities have near-zero state slopes (ΔB ≈ 0). Without rigorous counterfactual threat baselines or dedicated maintenance certificates, pure increment accounting fails to reward long-term guardianship of healthy ecosystems.

Wauchope et al. 2024 · Wunder et al. 2025
Pure Voluntary Contributions vs. Regulatory Offset Linkage

Voluntary corporate demand remains thin without compliance mandates; some propose allowing high-integrity credits into local regulatory markets, while conservation scientists warn that permitting cross-habitat substitution turns credits into licenses to destroy nature.

zu Ermgassen et al. 2019 · Wunder et al. 2025 · Peng et al. 2026
MRV Transaction Costs vs. Community Benefit Sharing

Multi-taxa monitoring (eDNA, bioacoustics, camera traps, remote sensing) and third-party auditing ensure scientific integrity but impose high fixed costs that can squeeze the share of funds reaching Indigenous Peoples and local communities (IPLCs).

Wauchope et al. 2024 · Peng et al. 2024, 2026

Connections

Flagship species
Voluntary buyer demand and price premiums are often driven by flagship charisma, though verification requires multi-taxa evidence (Peng et al. 2026).
Umbrella species
Crediting protocols (e.g., Savimbo jaguar credits, ERA protocol) use umbrella home ranges to bound project area and co-occurring gains (ERA 2025).
Keystone species
Trophic cascades and food-web functions driven by keystone species must be captured in multi-metric baskets rather than monoculture planting (Wauchope et al. 2024).
Indicator species
Basket-of-metrics accounting relies on multi-taxa indicator species to verify genuine recovery and prevent monoculture greenwashing (Wauchope et al. 2024).
