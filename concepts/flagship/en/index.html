Selection Criterion

Public Charisma

Chosen for popularity and emotional appeal to a target audience—not for trophic weight or home-range size.

Core Mechanism

Education & Funding

Acts as a visible conservation symbol that lowers cognitive barriers for education while inspiring donations.

Conceptual Boundary

Distinct from Umbrella & Keystone

A strategic marketing surrogate; it does not automatically act as an Umbrella species (habitat co-protection) or Keystone species.

References

  • Heywood (1995) Global Biodiversity Assessment (UNEP) — “Popular, charismatic species that serve as symbols and rallying points to stimulate conservation awareness and action.”
  • Simberloff (1998) Biological Conservation, 83(3), 247–257 — Distinguishes Flagship species (socio-political marketing symbol) from Umbrella species (habitat area) and Keystone species (trophic impact).
  • Walpole & Leader-Williams (2002) Biodiversity and Conservation, 11(3), 543–547 — “Their primary role is a strategic socio-economic one, to rally support and raise funds for conservation... charisma is the main criterion.”
  • Veríssimo et al. (2011) Conservation Letters, 4(1), 1–8 — “A species used as the focus of a broader conservation marketing campaign based on its possession of one or more traits that appeal to the target audience.”
